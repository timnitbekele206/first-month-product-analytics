    The short version

    Most people who start signing up do not finish.

    • 952 of 1,000 users visited the sign-up page. 101 confirmed their account (10.1%).
    • 37.0% came back after 7 days and 15.1% after 30 days.

    We also compared groups by device, region, referral source (how the user found the app), feature and subscription. None of the differences is strong enough to count as a finding. The section "Differences between groups" below explains why.

    Why this matters

    This is sample data. Read these as what the numbers would mean if they were real.

    • About 9 in 10 users never confirmed their account (899 of 1,000). Sign-up is where most users are lost.
    • Only 15.1% came back after 30 days, so most users would stop returning within the first month.
    • None of the group differences is strong enough to count as a finding. A team should not build a plan around Asia, Dark Mode or Export Data based on this sample. It would take more data to know.

    Where people drop out

    Of 1,000 users:

    • 952 visited the sign-up page.
    • 701 clicked.
    • 348 submitted the form.
    • 101 confirmed.

    What stands out:

    • Most users lost: click to submit, 353 users (half of those who clicked)
    • Biggest share lost: submit to confirm, 247 of 348 users (71%)

    Who comes back

    Among users with a recorded answer, the number who came back:

    • After 1 day: 698 of 986 (70.8%)
    • After 7 days: 366 of 989 (37.0%)
    • After 30 days: 150 of 994 (15.1%)

    The share fell 34 points from day 1 to day 7 and 22 points from day 7 to day 30.

    Differences between groups

    We compared groups to see whether the drop-off is concentrated in one place, such as one device, region or marketing channel. That meant 89 comparisons, and we saw four differences. With this many comparisons, some will appear by chance, so these may not be real. They are worth checking again in new data:

    • Users in Asia finished signing up less often: 6.3% (284 users) against 11.6% for everyone else.
    • Users whose feature was Dark Mode finished more often: 14.7% (218 users) against 8.8%.
    • Users whose feature was Export Data were less often subscribed: 30.9% (175 users) against 39.5%.
    • Users with no referral source finished at 23.8%, but there are only 21 of them. That is too few to draw a conclusion.

    Subscribers also finished signing up more often (12.3% of 373, against 8.9% of 610). This could be chance too.

    What this can't tell us

    • This is sample data. It says nothing about a real product or real people.
    • Each user has one feature in the data, and the data does not say why. Dark Mode users finishing more often does not mean Dark Mode caused it.
    • Some values are blank (6 to 17 users, depending on the measure). We left those out of each rate instead of counting them as no.
    • Rates for groups under 30 users are very uncertain.

    Recommended next steps

    • Find out why people leave at the last two sign-up steps. Click to submit and submit to confirm lose the most users. The data does not say why. Watching what happens at those steps or asking users who left would show it.
    • Check the four differences in new data. For example, compare the same four groups again using next month's sign-ups. Look at only these four, not all 89 again. If the same gaps appear, they are worth a closer look.
